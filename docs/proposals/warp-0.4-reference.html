WARP 0.4.0 reference

Every node type, every predicate group, and every slot on each. One page for looking things up: what a slot is called, what it ranges over, whether it is required, and whether it is part of what makes two statements the same proposition.

Companion to the proposals (warp-proposals-0.4.html), which argues for these additions; the build plan (warp-0.4-dev-guide.md), which sequences them; and the graph contract (docs/rdf-owl.md), which says what they become in RDF. This page only describes.

How to read this

Most of this is not built yet. WARP 0.3.0 has two predicate groups and twenty-one node templates. Everything marked 0.4 is proposed and lands across Phases 1 to 7 of the dev guide. Everything marked 0.3 exists today and was read off src/warp/schema/warp.yaml, templates/node_templates.yaml and the vendored upstreams rather than retyped from the proposals.

The classes are Statement, not Claim. The proposal pages write Claim, ClaimSet and RecommendationClaim; the code writes Statement, StatementSet and EcoStatement, and 0.4 keeps it that way. This page uses the code's names throughout, so RecommendationStatement where a proposal says RecommendationClaim.

A range in mono is a class or enum defined on this page unless it carries a prefix. CausalNode is CAMO's node record: it is the annotation record for every group's subjects and objects, and what kind of thing a node is comes from its template, never from the slot's range.

0.3 in the schema today 0.4 proposed required must be filled in key part of proposition identity derived written by a script, never an annotator
Still open.Of the questions in the dev guide, Q13 (SEPIO's example.org namespace), Q14 (what goes to Lars) and Q15 (Target 2 counting, release shape, Phase 9) are unanswered, and Q6 is marked "needs more detail". Nothing here depends on Q14 or Q15; Q6 is section 9; Q13 changes the IRIs in section 11 but no part of the model.

1. The shape of a record

One exchange file is a StatementSet. Inside it, four layers that refer to each other by id.

What statements are aboutNodesCausalNode records, each given a logical definition by a NodeDefinition that fills a template. Kinds of thing — except sites, which are particulars.
Who said whatStatementsSubject, predicate, object, plus scope, hedging, qualifiers, context and a claimant. One per source assertion.
What was saidPropositionsStatements that say the same thing, grouped under a versioned identity rule. Derived, never annotated.
What backs itEvidenceEvidence lines pointing at propositions, with study results, documents, agents and CARE metadata.

A statement is in SEPIO Assertion Mode: it puts a proposition forward as true, and leaves direction, strength and score empty. SynthesisAssessment is the one class in Proposition Assessment Mode — it is about the state of the evidence for a proposition, so it fills those slots instead.

Nothing in a statement set is asserted as true of the world. It records that someone said something, about something, on some basis.

2. Node types

What statements are about. A node is a kind of thing — a variable, an action, a population — with one exception, the site, which is a particular.

A node has two records. The CausalNode is the annotation record: an id, a name, an entity type and the CAMO fields an extraction pass fills. The NodeDefinition gives it a logical definition by naming a template and filling that template's variables with ontology terms. expand_nodes.py turns each filled template into one OWL class with a generated label, a text definition and an equivalent-class axiom.

Leave direction of change out of the node. "Increased", "decreased" and "removed" are not part of what the node is; the predicate carries the change. That is what lets a statement of increase and a statement of decrease meet on one node.

Every 0.3 template has status: draft. A template's logical definition is a nested class expression: a genus, intersected with existential restrictions whose fillers are again class expressions. Unions, negation, data properties and list variables are not supported by the generator.

management_strategy 0.4

A set of management actions carried out together, in a stated order, so that a cost, an outcome or a recommendation can point at the bundle without being divided among its parts. Its parts are references to ordinary action nodes; the strategy holds only their order.

The object of a tolerance statement: a quality (PATO) of an environmental material or medium (ENVO), optionally at a depth interval and a season. It must cover water and climate variables — water depth, velocity, temperature — and not only soil.

site 0.4

The place a strategy is carried out, or whose condition is assessed. A node, so any group can point at it — but unlike every other node a particular: one piece of ground, not a kind of thing. Sites are OWL individuals under their own site: prefix, never classes, and geometry never goes into a generated output.

Finding a URI for a place.place takes an IRI, not a name, and the point of it is that a statement can be about a municipality, a watershed or a treaty area rather than an unclear radius around a point. In order of preference: an ISO 3166-2 code for a province or territory (CA-SK); a Wikidata item for anything with a settled identity — a municipality, a park, a lake, a First Nation's territory (the wd: prefix is already declared in the profile); a gazetteer entry where Wikidata has none, such as GeoNames or the Getty Thesaurus of Geographic Names; and failing all three, a warp:place/ concept minted locally with a label and whatever it nests inside, plus a TODO to replace it. Never a bare string, and never a coordinate pair standing in for an area.

4. Predicate types

Seven groups after 0.4. Each is one kind of statement: its predicates, its qualifiers, the statement-level slots it adds, and what makes two of its statements the same proposition.

One restoration, eight statements. The first three are in the schema today, read from examples/bloodroot_statements.yaml; the five below them are illustrative, written to show what each proposed group would hold about the same work. Rows 1 and 2 have the same subject, predicate and object and differ only in scope, so they derive two propositions, not one. Row 3 is negated, so it exports no relation triple. Figures, sites and the criteria set are placeholders.

A group is registered by four annotations on its statement class — warp_group, warp_statements_slot, warp_propositions_slot, warp_proposition_class — and the derivation scripts find it from those, so adding a group edits no script.

A project is two records

This is the part marked "needs more detail" in the dev guide, so it is worth spelling out. When three organizations report the same restoration work, derivation produces two things, and they are different kinds of thing.

Record · information entityImplementationPropositionThe grouping: which statements were merged, each claimant's area and status, the union of implementers and funders, the envelope of periods. It holds the disagreement rather than resolving it — 42 ha from the land trust and 40 ha from the funder, both attributed. Like every proposition, it is a record of what was said.
Individual · occurrentproj:<id>The work: an instance of the strategy class, at the site individual, over the period. It is a thing that happened in the world, not a record about it. The proposition is IAO:0000136 (is about) this individual.

The distinction matters for exactly one reason: a criteria statement has to attach to one of them. "This project qualifies as effective restoration" is a judgement about the work that was done — the earthworks, the seeding, the three years of mowing on 42 hectares. It is not a judgement about a grouping record. Earlier drafts put the verdict on the proposition (subject_proposition), which reads as "this merge of three reports qualifies as effective restoration", and that is not what any assessor means.

Giving the work its own individual also has two practical payoffs. It is what makes the strategy class worth having: proj:0412 rdf:type node:earthworks-seed-mow says this particular episode was of that kind, which is the only point at which the node layer and the real world touch. And it means criteria statements have one subject slot with one kind of filler — a particular, whether a site or a project — so the exactly_one_of rule and the alternative subject slot both disappear from the schema.

What you would see.Two reports of the same agreement derive one ImplementationProposition listing both statements with their areas, and one proj:0412 individual typed by the strategy class and located at site:0412. A criteria statement then reads subject: proj:0412. If the two reports had no shared external id, no shared site or no overlapping period, they would stay two propositions and two project individuals, which is the honest answer when nothing says they are the same work.

A statement becomes an OWL class axiom only when its proposition has scope: universal, is not negated, every statement under it has modality: observed, and a SynthesisAssessment supports it. On the examples that exist today that is nothing, which is the right answer: prototypical means "typically, allowing exceptions", and a subclass axiom cannot say that.
