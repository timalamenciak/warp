WARP 0.4 proposals: consolidated guidance

Five draft proposals in one reference: three new predicate groups (recommendation, tolerance, cost), a management strategy node type, and sites, implementation and definitions for restoration reporting. Read with the build plan in docs/dev-guide-0.4.md.

Compiled from the proposal pages of 7 to 11 November 2026. Where the proposals or their schema sketches disagreed, this page uses the harmonized form from the dev guide (see section 11). Predicate names, qualifier values, example node IDs and all figures are provisional.

Revised 20 November 2026: the proposals said what each group records but not what any of it becomes in RDF. Section 4 is the contract that settles it, and each group now says what it exports and what, if anything, it projects to OWL. Four of the problems it fixes are in 0.3.0 already, and seven groups would have multiplied them.

1. Overview

WARP records sourced, scoped statements: who said it, subject → predicate → object, and what backs it. Statements that say the same thing are grouped into propositions by a script, under a versioned identity rule. Version 0.3.0 has two predicate groups, causal and interaction. These proposals add five more, plus three node templates, so WARP can also hold guidance, environmental tolerances, costs, and records of restoration that actually took place.

Each new group answers a question the existing groups cannot hold without losing what makes the statement what it is: the advice in guidance, the range in a tolerance, the context of a cost, the fact that work was done.

Every group also says what it becomes in RDF, and whether any of it becomes OWL. Section 4 is that contract, and it is worth reading before the groups: it settles the IRI spaces, where predicates are declared, where polarity lives, and the gate a claim has to pass before a reasoner is allowed to use it. Four of the groups project nothing to OWL, on purpose.

2. The annotator's tests

Every proposal comes with one question that tells an annotator where a sentence belongs. When a sentence passes two tests, record two statements and link them.

Recommendation or causal

Is the source telling someone what to do, or describing what happens?

"Do X to achieve Y" is guidance. "The outcome of X is, or was, Y" is causal. When a sentence does both, record both; the guidance cites the causal proposition through justified_by.

Strategy or part

Does the source report this for the actions together, or for each one?

"Recontouring and seeding the site cost $48,000" is about the strategy. "Seeding added $9,000" is about one part. Never divide a strategy-level figure among its parts.

Tolerance or causal

Is the source saying under what conditions the organism can live, or what changes what?

"Grows on soils of pH 5.5 to 8" is tolerance. "Compaction reduces root penetration" is causal. A tolerance explained by a mechanism links to the causal proposition through explained_by.

Cost or causal

Is the source saying what an action took, or what it did?

"Treating 600 ha took two seasons and cost NIS 17,600" is cost. "Treatment led to regeneration of native vegetation" is causal. The cost statement points at the causal statements through outcome_reported.

Implementation or recommendation

Does the source say this was done, or that it should be done?

"We recontoured and seeded 42 ha in 2024" is implementation. "Recontouring is recommended for sites like this" is a recommendation. Scheduled work is implementation with status: planned only if it is committed to a specific site.

Criteria

Is someone applying a named, versioned definition to a site or project?

"Degraded" with no definition named is context, not a criteria claim. A criteria claim always names its criteria set and the period assessed.

4. The graph: RDF and OWL

WARP's output is RDF, and OWL wherever the content allows it. Five groups arriving at once is the moment to say which of them produces what, because a predicate, an enum or an IRI space is cheap to settle now and expensive to revisit seven groups later.

The five proposals say what each group records. None of them says what any of it becomes in RDF, and four of the places where 0.3.0 already produces something that is not OWL 2 would be multiplied by seven: predicate IRIs declared as classes, enum slots declared as object properties but carrying literals, node IRIs used as both class and individual, and polarity kept in a sibling slot where a triple cannot see it. This section is the contract that fixes them. The dev guide carries it as decisions D11 to D20 and builds it in Phase 2.5, before the groups.

Four IRI spaces, and nothing crosses between them. A node: IRI is a class everywhere it appears; a site: or proj: IRI is an individual everywhere. The prefixes are what let a check catch a mistake, instead of leaving one IRI quietly meaning two things.

The projection gate

Before any claim becomes an OWL axiom

Is this true of every instance, said plainly, by someone, and supported?

A proposition projects only when all four hold: scope is universal; negated is false; every claim grouped under it has modality: observed; and a SynthesisAssessment supports it. Everything else stays in the reification layer, where it can still be queried, reported and synthesized.

The condition that bites is scope. prototypical — what typically holds, allowing exceptions — is the usual scope of an ecological generalization and the one WARP was built to record. OWL 2 has no way to say "typically": a subclass axiom is exceptionless, so projecting a prototypical claim would state something stronger than its source did. On the examples in this page the gate passes nothing, and that is the right answer.

What never reaches OWL, whatever its scope: guidance, because "you should do X" has no OWL rendering; ranges, optima, curves, amounts and efforts, because they are measurements of populations and episodes, not class axioms; criteria verdicts, because a criteria set is a stated test and not a definition; step order, lags and repeats; and anything negated.

Polarity in the predicate

"Do prescribed burning" and "avoid prescribed burning" are two propositions, because guidance is in the identity key. With one predicate they are also two identical triples, and the distinction the recommendation group exists to keep is lost the moment anyone reads the relation layer without the qualifiers. So polarity goes in the predicate. The qualifier stays — it is what annotators fill in — and the exporter picks the sub-property from it.

Cost is the same move, for a different reason."This action incurs_cost total" is not a relation between an action and a category: a cost is three things at once, and the only part of it that is a relation is action-to-amount. Putting the category in the predicate leaves the amount as the object, where it belongs, and means no costcat: IRIs have to be minted for things nothing can say the kind of.

Adding a group

Five questions, answered in the group's own section and recorded in docs/rdf-owl.md. The seventh group should be no harder than the third.

  1. Which predicates does it mint? Declared once in warp-relations.ttl, as object properties, with the proposal's definitions.
  2. What kind of thing is each end? A class, an individual, or a value. Where it varies by predicate — has_habitat takes an ENVO habitat while the other tolerance predicates take a quality node — no LinkML range and no OWL axiom can say so, and a script checks it.
  3. Which qualifier carries polarity? If one does, it becomes sub-properties.
  4. What does it export? Which individuals, with which IRIs, and which values need IRIs of their own because something else will cite them.
  5. What does it project? The default, and the answer for four of the seven groups, is nothing.
Predicate group · proposed 7 November 2026

5. The recommendation group

Guidance: statements in which a source tells someone to do, avoid or consider an action because of an outcome. One predicate, suggested_to.

Prompted by two SARA recovery strategies: Spring Salamander, Adirondack / Appalachian population (proposed, 2026) and Mountain Sucker, Milk River populations (final, 2026). Their recovery planning tables are guidance, and neither existing group can hold them.

Why a new group

Recovery strategies, management plans and practitioner guides mix two kinds of statement. Some describe how the world works: sediment fills the spaces between streambed stones, so salamanders lose cover. Others tell the reader what to do: rationalize the sportfish stocking program; prevent baitfish introductions. The first is judged by its evidence. The second is judged by whether it fits the goal and the place, and depends on statements of the first kind.

Recording guidance as a hedged causal statement loses the advice, its strength, its priority and who gave it. Recording it as a causal qualifier mixes judgements about what to do into the description of the world. A separate group keeps the two apart and links them.

The subject is the action as the source names it; the object is the outcome as the source names it; the advice goes in a qualifier. This avoids double negatives ("avoid stocking" suggested_to "prevent decrease") and avoids minting avoidance nodes that would never match the plain action node. The predicate is read in the passive, so the subject is never the recommender; who made the suggestion is the claimant, as in every other group.

Proposition identity

The key adds guidance to the usual fields. Without it, "do prescribed burning, it reduces shrub cover" from a grassland plan and "avoid prescribed burning, it reduces shrub cover" from an agency worried about smoke would derive one proposition, and the guidance would be lost. With it, they become two propositions citing the same causal proposition: agreement about how the world works, disagreement about what to do. When two agencies give the same guidance, their statements derive one proposition with two claimants.

Predictions and unsupported guidance

A prediction such as "if predacious sportfish are stocked, Mountain Sucker abundance will decrease" describes what happens, so it is a causal statement with modality: anticipated, hedging in causal_language, usually scoped to a particular population and period. The guidance cites it through justified_by. Keeping them apart lets someone accept the prediction and still reject the advice.

Annotation rule. When a guidance sentence states an outcome, record the causal prediction as well. When it gives no outcome or reason, leave justified_by empty. Predictions are never derived automatically from guidance: that would put claims in the source's mouth.

SituationExampleHow it is recorded
Expert opinionMountain Sucker threat ratings: certainty set by expert opinion onlyEvidence line, evidence_type: expert_judgment

Explicitly unknownSpring Salamander: the impact of road salt cannot be properly assessedOpen-question proposition, not a prediction

Self-supporting guidance. When guidance cites a prediction whose only support is a not_stated or expert_judgment statement by the same claimant, the guidance supports itself: DFO advises avoiding stocking because DFO predicts harm. This is not an error; in recovery planning it is often the precautionary principle at work. It is flagged so TREK can show it as guidance resting on assertion, which is where effectiveness monitoring adds the most.

In the graph

Exported: one relation triple per claim, using the sub-property for its guidance value, plus the reified claim with its force, priority, timing and contribution. priority and timing_of_influence carry scale and concept IRIs from the DFO and ECCC schemes, not bare tokens.

Projected to OWL: nothing, whatever the scope. Guidance is deontic, and "you should do X" has no OWL 2 rendering; a graph that cannot tell advice from description is worse than one that holds only description. The causal prediction a recommendation cites through justified_by may project on its own, if it passes the gate — which keeps exactly the separation this group was created for.

What this makes possible

  • Weakly supported guidance. Guidance whose justified_by points to a causal proposition with a low synthesis grade, or to nothing.
  • Self-supporting guidance. Guidance whose only justification is the same claimant's unsupported prediction, where monitoring effort is best spent.
  • Guidance to revisit. When a causal proposition's assessment changes, flag every recommendation that cites it.
  • Effectiveness monitoring as evidence. Five-year progress reports produce causal statements that support or dispute the propositions the guidance cites.
  • Agreement and disagreement across plans. Shared guidance gathers in one proposition; opposite guidance on the same action and outcome sits side by side.

Open questions

  1. Claimant for government documents. The Minister, the recovery team or the department? It matters for CARE metadata where Indigenous knowledge informs the strategy.
  2. Priority scales. Keep each agency's scale (proposed), or also map to a shared ordinal for querying?
  3. Research actions. "Clarify life history" aims to close a knowledge gap, so its natural object is an open-question proposition. Allow a proposition as object, or model the gap as a node?
  4. Conditions on guidance. "Avoid in-stream work during egg incubation": is context enough, or is a structured condition slot needed?
  5. Scope. Guidance about one named population over the next ten years is about a particular and looks forward. Check with Lars how semantic unit types handle it.
  6. Theories of change. Map suggested_to chains to CMP Open Standards results chains so practitioners see a familiar structure?
Node type · proposed 8 November 2026

6. The management strategy node

A node type for a set of management actions carried out together, in a stated order. Statements in any group can point at the strategy as a whole, while its parts stay as separate, reusable action nodes.

Prompted by the cost group. ELMO's actions are atomic and a node carries one entity_term, but sources usually report costs, outcomes and recommendations for the whole bundle. Splitting those statements across parts would invent attributions the source never made.

The problem

ELMO keeps hand seeding and earthworks as separate terms, which is right for an ontology of actions. Restoration practice combines them: a project report gives one cost for recontouring and seeding; a recovery strategy recommends a package of in-stream works; a field study compares two treatment sequences. Without a strategy node WARP has three poor options: attach the statement to one part (misattribution), copy it to every part (multiplying one cost or effect), or mint a free-text node that matches nothing and loses which actions are involved.

Illustrative strategy and figures. Each part is an ordinary action node with its own ELMO term and parameters, such as the seed mix. Two strategies that differ only in seed mix have different seeding components, so they are different strategies.

"Strategy" matches the CMP Open Standards, where a strategy is a group of actions aimed at results, and matches how recovery strategies use the word.

Identity

Under warp-strategy-identity/1, two strategy definitions are the same node when their required components and step pattern match. The key is the sorted list of (step, action) pairs for non-optional components, hashed, after flattening nested strategies one level. Lags, repeats, roles, labels and optional parts are aggregated, not keyed. Two sources describing earthworks then hand seeding with the same mix meet at one node, whatever they call it; adding mowing makes a different strategy.

Annotators give strategies local IDs. Derivation computes the canonical ID and rewrites references, and it runs before proposition derivation, so statements about two descriptions of the same work meet in one proposition.

Because parts are references, the graph answers containment questions directly: every strategy that includes hand seeding, strategies that are this one plus one step, strategies that differ only in order. Syntheses can pool evidence for one action across all strategies that contain it, while marking that the effect was measured on a bundle.

OWL and DOSDP

The composition renders in OWL 2 EL as subsumption, not equivalence: node:S rdfs:subClassOf planned process, and one rdfs:subClassOf has_part some A per required component, with the BFO occurrent part relation. Order, lags, repeats and roles do not render; they stay in the reification layer, where each component gets its own IRI so the order is addressable. No DOSDP pattern: part counts vary, so the OWL is generated directly from the definitions, and this is the first template whose OWL comes from outside the template machinery.

Earlier drafts said equivalent class. That would be wrong, and the identity rule is why. The key is the sorted list of (step, action) pairs, so order is part of identity: earthworks-then-seeding and seeding-then-earthworks are two strategy nodes. Their part axioms are identical, because order does not render. Asserted as equivalence, those axioms tell a reasoner the two nodes are the same class — contradicting the rule that just held them apart. Asserted as subsumption they say only what is true: this strategy has these parts.

Nothing is lost. Containment was the only thing equivalence was wanted for, and subsumption gives it: the query class has_part some hand_seeding has every strategy containing hand seeding among its subclasses. The part relation is declared once, as transitive, and used only inside some, never in a cardinality restriction, which keeps the output in EL — and transitivity is also what makes the derivation's one-level flattening agree with the OWL, since a nested strategy's parts are parts of the outer strategy either way.

Canonical IDs are content hashes.Change a component and the strategy's class IRI changes. When derivation supersedes an ID the generated output carries owl:deprecated and IAO:0100001 (term replaced by) on the old IRI, because a published OWL class IRI that silently moves is worse than one that is formally retired. Strategy class IRIs are stable only while their parts are.

7. The tolerance group

The conditions under which a taxon establishes, persists or performs: the soil pH it tolerates, the moisture regime it prefers, the salinity that excludes it. The building blocks of habitat suitability.

Prompted by a practitioner who builds habitat suitability models from edaphic factors in a graph. WARP can currently reach suitability only indirectly, by treating "suitability of a site for a taxon" as a node.

Why a new group

A causal statement says a change in one thing brings a change in another. A tolerance statement describes a disposition of the organism: across a range of conditions it can live here and not there, and does best somewhere in between. Forcing tolerance into the causal group goes wrong in three ways:

  • The shape is wrong. causal_direction assumes a monotonic relation. Edaphic responses are usually unimodal or threshold-shaped, and the range, which is the content, would end up in free text.
  • The object is a model construct. "Suitability of site for taxon" cannot be measured; it is the output of a model, so causal edges into it mix evidence with the model's combination rule.
  • The evidence reads as weak. Tolerance knowledge comes from where species occur, expert ranking or growth trials across a gradient. As causal evidence that looks correlational and low-grade; as tolerance evidence much of it is well supported.

In the graph

Exported: the relation for every claim, with range, optimum, class value, response and curve on the claim's own qualifier individual. Class values carry scale and concept IRIs from project/scales.ttl; a practitioner's "wet" carries no scale, because it is not on one.

Ranges, optima, class values and curves never enter OWL at all — they are measurements across populations, not class axioms, and this is where the disposition question (Q2) now sits: a disposition rendering would need the range as a datatype facet, which leaves OWL 2 EL and buys nothing a query over the reification layer does not already give.

What this makes possible

  • Site–species matching. Join tolerance propositions with a site's soil data; a seed-mix recommendation cites them through justified_by.

  • Conflicting ranges flag where sources disagree, often over response or region.
  • Priors for models: ranges and optima for SDMs and BBN tables; fitted models return as correlation-type evidence.
  • Practitioner and Indigenous knowledge beside databases, each with its own claimant and care.

8. The cost group

What an action took to carry out (money, staff and volunteer time, equipment and materials) with the context needed to read the number. Qualifiers follow the five cost reporting standards of Iacona et al. (2018).

Prompted by the decision support sensitivity analysis. Causal statements give the benefit side of cost-effectiveness; nothing yet gives the cost side. Costs are highly context dependent, so every cost is a scoped statement by a claimant, and any reuse in a new context is an explicit, recorded step.

Why a new group

A cost statement says that carrying out an action, at a given scale, place and year, used a given amount of resources. It is not a claim about how the world responds, so direction and strength do not fit. Its truth depends on accounts and records, so the evidence types that grade causal claims would grade it as weak when it is often exact.

A cost figure is also almost meaningless without context. Iacona and colleagues reviewed 30 published costings, found critical information routinely missing, and proposed five reporting standards. WARP turns those standards into required and recommended slots, so a cost cannot enter the graph stripped of what it needs.

Standard 1Objective and outcomeWhat the spending aimed at, and what it achieved
Standard 2Context and methodApproach, starting conditions, intensity, phase
Standard 3When, where, scalePlace, period, area or units treated
Standard 4Cost categoriesLabour, capital and equipment, consumables, overhead
Standard 5Currency and dateOriginal currency, year, conversion, adjustment

For the sensitivity analysis

  • Ranges, not points. Vary cost across each proposition's spread and see whether the preferred option changes.
  • Cost-effectiveness with traceable parts: a cost proposition divided by a causal effect, both traced to sources.
  • Filters by basis: rerun with actual expenditure only, or without estimates and transferred costs.
  • Value of information: where the decision changes across the cost range, that cost is a priority for collection.

9. Sites, implementation and definitions

Three additions that let WARP record restoration that actually happened, where it happened, and whether it counts as restoring a degraded area, without fixing any one definition of "degraded" or "effective" into the graph.

Prompted by two challenges under Target 2 of Canada's 2030 Nature Strategy: no national definition of "degraded areas" or "effective restoration", and information on restoration efforts scattered across many organizations.

The problem

None of the other groups records that a strategy was carried out at a place, over a period, by someone. Restoration reporting needs exactly that, aggregated across organizations that each hold part of the picture and often report the same project twice.

Reporting also depends on two contested judgements: whether an area is degraded, and whether restoration there is effective. If WARP built one definition into its structure, every record would need redoing when the definition changed, and nobody could see how much the choice matters.

Illustrative IDs and figures. Solid boxes are nodes; the dashed box is derived; criteria sets are information records, not nodes. Dashed amber lines are criteria claims.

Site

The place a strategy is carried out, or whose condition is assessed. A node, so any group can point at it, but unlike every node so far a particular: one piece of ground, not a kind of thing. In OWL, sites are individuals, not classes, and geometry never goes into generated outputs.

Sites therefore get their own IRI space, site:, rather than sharing node: (section 4). The prefix is doing real work: node: IRIs are classes everywhere they appear and site: IRIs are individuals everywhere, so a check can catch a mistake instead of leaving one IRI meaning two things depending on which file you read. Only the IRI space differs; the LinkML range stays CausalNode, as in every other group.

Projects are derived

Under warp-impl-identity/1, two implementation claims describe the same project when they share an external ID, or when their strategy matches, their sites match and their periods overlap. A land trust, its funder and a provincial program reporting the same work become three attributed claims about one project. Where they disagree on area or status, the disagreement is visible rather than silently resolved or double-counted.

Site matching is the hard part: hash identity works for strategies but not for polygons, and generalized geometry makes it harder. Version 1 matches only on shared site IDs and external IDs, and reports candidate spatial matches for review. Periods overlap by normalized bounds, not by string comparison: the claims below say 2024-05 and 2023, which no string ordering handles.

Earlier drafts wrote ImplementationProposition # = project, which makes an information record the same thing as the work that was done — and then made an effectiveness verdict an assertion about that record. The proposition stays what it is, the derived grouping record with its member claims and aggregates, and derivation also mints the project itself.

A project is an occurrent: something that happened, at a place, over a period. Typing it by its strategy class is what connects the two layers — the class says what kind of work it was, the individual says that this work occurred here. It also gives the criteria group a subject that is a particular, so "this project qualifies as effective restoration" is about the work and not about a record of it.

The project individual is this group's logical-layer output, and it comes from the derivation rule, not from the gate.

Definitions are not nodes

A definition such as "degraded area, version 1, issued by a federal department" is a particular, like documents, claims and identity rules, but nodes are what statements are about. A definition is closer to an identity rule: a stated, versioned test that other records are checked against. So definitions become criteria sets, information records named in a qualifier on claims that apply them.

ConceptWhat is being definedControlled vocabulary (SKOS): degraded_area, effective_restoration. Stable across definitions.

The criteria group

One predicate for annotators, warp:qualifies_as, and two in the graph. Subject: a site individual (degradation) or a project individual (effectiveness) — both particulars, so subject is simply required; object: a concept; the criteria set is a required qualifier. Under warp-criteria-identity/1, two criteria claims share a proposition when subject, concept, criteria set and assessed period match. "Qualifies under A" and "does not qualify under B" are different propositions and do not contradict. Two assessors applying the same definition and disagreeing share a proposition, one supporting and one disputing it. The verdict is the claim's evidential direction: supports, disputes, neutral.

Earlier drafts held a project subject in subject_proposition, with exactly one of the two required. With the project as an individual (above) that is unnecessary: both kinds of subject are particulars, the identity rule needs no alternative subject slot, and a verdict is about the work rather than about a record of it.

In the graph: the object is the criteria set, not the concept.The exported triple is site:0412 warp:qualifies_as crit:degraded-area/fed-draft-1, with the concept recoverable through the set's operationalizes. Keeping the set in the object position is what stops the graph holding site:0412 qualifies_as degraded_area and site:0412 does_not_qualify_as degraded_area as a flat contradiction, when the two verdicts are in fact about two definitions. The verdict comes from the claim's direction: supports gives qualifies_as, disputes gives does_not_qualify_as, both under an abstract warp:assessed_against, and a neutral claim exports no relation triple at all. This is the one place where the exported object is not the annotated object.

Projected to OWL: nothing. A criteria set is a stated, versioned test, deliberately not an OWL definition, so a verdict can never be inferred — which is also exactly what lets a definition change without a single record being rewritten. The concepts are skos:Concept and never owl:Class, and the enum's meaning: points at those same IRIs so that one concept does not end up with two. This also answers half of the automated-verdicts question (Q6): a SHACL or SPARQL rule may propose a verdict, and it enters as a claim by EcoWeaver with assessment_method: rule_applied, like any other claim, never as an axiom. Whether a policy counts such verdicts is not ours to settle.

The Target 2 query

For a jurisdiction and year, under definitions D (degraded) and E (effective):

  1. Sites with a supported qualifies_as degraded_area proposition under D, for the baseline period.
  2. Derived projects on those sites with status in_progress, completed or maintained in the reporting year.
  3. Of those, projects with a supported qualifies_as effective_restoration proposition under E.
  4. The union, not the sum, of their treated areas, since projects on one site can overlap. Without geometry this is reported as lower and upper bounds.

Run it again with another D or E and the difference is the effect of the definition. WARP does not settle which definition is right; it shows what each would mean for the national number, and which records would change.

Open questions

  1. Particulars in the node layer. Should sites be nodes, or a separate collection like documents? The mechanics are settled — individuals, under their own prefix, never classes — so what is left for Lars is whether they belong in the node layer at all.
  2. Spatial identity. When do two site records describe the same place? v1: shared IDs only; spatial overlap produces candidates for review.
  3. Statements about propositions. Answered: neither. The subject is the project individual, so no claim is made about a record and no parallel assessment class is needed.
  4. Element vocabulary. SER's six attributes, the IUCN Red List of Ecosystems criteria, FAO's FERM, or whatever the federal process settles on?
  5. Counting rules. Does planned work count as "under restoration"? Area treated or site area?
  6. Automated verdicts. Criteria sets expressed as SHACL or SPARQL rules: should rule_applied verdicts count for reporting, or only flag candidates? How they enter is settled — as a claim by EcoWeaver, never as an axiom — but whether a policy counts them is not ours to settle.
  7. Existing platforms. Map to FERM and CMP Open Standards so registries can be imported.
  8. Reporting without exposure. Can roll-ups run over restricted records and publish only aggregates, and who authorizes that under care?
