For CAMO annotators

WARP for people who know CAMO

If you've been annotating CausalEdges, most of what you know carries straight over: the same nodes, predicates, causal features, comparators and evidence types. What changes is where things sit. This page shows what moved, what's new, and which habits to change.

Data model, not the form This page is about how WARP stores an annotation. How Loom will present it (for example, keeping the familiar CAMO edge form and writing WARP statements behind it) hasn't been decided yet. Whatever the form looks like, the decisions described here are the ones you'll be making.

The short version

In CAMO you…In WARP you…
annotate a CausalEdge in a CausalGraphannotate a CausalStatement in a StatementSet
put every causal feature directly on the edgeput them in one block, predicate_qualifiers
choose token_or_typechoose scope from four values plus unclear, and it matters more
may give nodes a state qualifier ("increased…")name the variable; the predicate carries the sign
list evidence_types and evidence_objectswrite one evidence line per evidence type, each with its object, direction and strength
are the edge's annotatorare the annotator; the source's authors are the author
(no equivalent)get a proposition filled in automatically, grouping your statement with matching ones
see EvidenceBaseAssessmentEdge for synthesessee SynthesisAssessment, which cites statements as evidence

Why the edge was split up

A CAMO edge mixes four kinds of information that belong to different things:

InformationBelongs toCAMO examplesWARP home
What relation is claimedthe relation itselfpredicate, necessity, reversibility, mediation…predicate + predicate_qualifiers
How it was saidthe speakercausal_language, original_sentencethe statement
What it's aboutthe ideatoken_or_typescope, part of the proposition
What backs itthe evidenceevidential_basis, certainty_gradeevidence lines and assessments

Separating them buys three things CAMO can't do on its own: grouping edges from different papers that make the same claim (propositions), citing one person's statement as evidence for another's (evidence lines), and recognising practitioners and Indigenous knowledge holders as people who make statements rather than only as evidence. WARP gets the statement/proposition/evidence frame from SEPIO, and keeps CAMO for everything causal.

What stays the same

WARP imports CAMO unchanged. Everything below uses CAMO's own enums and classes, with the same values you already know:

  • Nodes are still CausalNodes: name, entity_type, entity_term, measured_attribute, applied_to, source_spans. (Interaction statements use them too, despite the name.)
  • Predicates: CausalPredicateEnum, all eleven, same meanings and Rosetta templates.
  • Causal language: direct_causal, uncertain_causal, associational, no_relationship.
  • Negation: same rule. negated: true if and only if causal_language: no_relationship, and you still never encode a null result with an "unchanged" node. The WARP checks enforce this and reject edges that break it.
  • Philosophical accounts and the causal features: same names, same values, same FeatureAssertionEnum (explicitly_asserted, implicitly_assumed, explicitly_denied, not_addressed).
  • Structured annotations: DirectionAnnotation, MediationAnnotation, ModerationAnnotation, StrengthAnnotation, ComparatorAnnotation (still one per arm), ContextAnnotation, TemporalExtent, TextSpan.
  • Ecosystem context: IUCN GET functional groups via ELMO.
  • Evidence types and objects: EvidenceTypeEnum and EvidenceObjectEnum, same values.
  • Certainty grades and evidence balance: CertaintyGradeEnum, EvidenceBalanceEnum.

      dateRecordCreated: "2026-10-10T14:00:00Z"
      contributions:

           role: annotator, contributor: "orcid:0000-0000-0000-0000"}

The statement is longer mainly because of provenance: who made it and who recorded it are now two separate, credited things. The causal content is identical.

token_or_type becomes scope

This is the biggest change to your day-to-day decisions. type is split into three, and the choice now decides which other edges yours is grouped with. A statement with scope missing or unclear isn't grouped at all until someone resolves it.

You'd have chosenNow askScope
tokenAbout this plot, this population, this burn?assertional
typeHolds usually, with exceptions? (The default: most ecological generalizations.)prototypical
Can or sometimes does happen ("may", "can", "in some cases")?contingent
Holds without exception? (Rare: needs the source to say so.)universal
ambiguous, not_addressedReally can't tell?unclear
Don't confuse "can" with hedging "Bloodroot may reduce garlic mustard" is usually hedging (causal_language: uncertain_causal) about a typical effect, so prototypical. "Bloodroot can reduce garlic mustard (under some conditions)" is a claim about some cases, so contingent. Hedging is how sure the author is; scope is how many cases they mean.

Existing CAMO edges are migrated as token → assertional, type → prototypical, and flagged for a human to check. You're the right person to check them.

Nodes: drop the state qualifier

The CausalNode record is unchanged, but WARP's convention is that a node is a variable, not a change. In the worked example the effect node is "Population growth rate of Alliaria petiolata" with no state_or_change_qualifier, and the predicate prevents carries the sign.

CAMO habitWARP
planting → causes → decreased GM growth rateplanting → prevents → GM growth rate
burning → causes → increased forb coverburning → causes → forb cover

Why it matters more now: propositions are grouped by exact node id. "Increased X" and "decreased X" as separate nodes would put a positive finding and a negative finding in different propositions, so a synthesis would never see them disagree. With one node, they meet.

Node templates

New in WARP: a node can also have a node definition saying which template it fills and with which ontology terms. That turns it into an OWL class with a generated label. Three templates exist (population_of_taxon, quality_of_process_of_population, intervention_involving_taxon).

node_definitions:
  - node: node:rate_of_population_growth_of_alliaria_petiolata
    template: quality_of_process_of_population
    fillers:
      - {var: quality, value: "PATO:0000161", label: rate}
      - {var: process, value: "PCO:0000011", label: population growth}
      - {var: taxon,   value: "NCBITaxon:126270", label: Alliaria petiolata}

Note the taxon: NCBITaxon in the template filling, while entity_term keeps the Wikidata QID as in CAMO. Both are kept; a crosswalk between them is planned. The template's camo_entity_type must match the node's entity_type, or the node expansion fails.

evidential_basis becomes evidence lines

In CAMO, evidence_types and evidence_objects are two independent lists. In WARP each evidence type gets its own evidence line, and each line has exactly one type and one object. So you now decide which object goes with which type.

# CAMO
evidential_basis:
  evidence_types: [randomized_experiment, mechanistic_study]
  evidence_objects: [correlation, mechanism]

# WARP
hasEvidenceLines:
  - {evidence_type: randomized_experiment, evidence_object: correlation,
     directionOfEvidenceProvided: supports, strengthOfEvidenceProvided: moderate, …}
  - {evidence_type: mechanistic_study, evidence_object: mechanism,
     directionOfEvidenceProvided: supports, strengthOfEvidenceProvided: weak, …}

Each line also asks for things CAMO didn't ask per article:

FieldWhat you decide
directionOfEvidenceProvided (required)Does this evidence support, dispute or stay neutral toward the statement? For a paper's own evidence for its own claim, nearly always supports.
strengthOfEvidenceProvidedstrong, moderate, weak or not_assessed. Use not_assessed if you haven't been asked to judge it.
evidence_item_refsIds of what the evidence is: study results, the document, other statements.
contributions (optional)Who judged direction and strength, with role evidence_assessor. Leave it out and the judgement is attributed to you as the annotator.

You never fill targetProposition on your statement's evidence lines: like the proposition, it's derived.

Annotator vs author

CAMO has one person on an edge: you, the annotator. WARP separates the person who made the statement from the person who recorded it.

RoleWhoWhere it goes
authorThe paper's authors, the practitioner, the knowledge holderthe statement's contributions
annotatorYourecordMetadata.contributions
evidence_assessorWhoever judged an evidence line (you, by default)the evidence line's contributions
reviewerSomeone checking your workwherever they reviewed

Everyone (authors, you, the synthesis pipeline) is an EcoAgent in the set's agents list, with an agent_kind: researcher, practitioner, indigenous_knowledge_holder, community_member, organization, synthesis_pipeline. Contributions point at agents by id. The checks reject a role in the wrong place (e.g. author inside recordMetadata), and warn if a statement has no author or no annotator.

Edges you don't write: propositions

CAMO has no object for "the claim that planting bloodroot prevents garlic mustard growth" independent of any paper. WARP does: the proposition. A script groups statements into propositions when these match exactly:

group · subject node · predicate · object node · negated · scope

Everything else (causal language, features, comparator, context, evidence) stays on the statement and is compared across statements, not used for grouping. You never write the proposition field.

What this means for annotation:

  • Consistent node ids matter. Reusing the existing node is what lets your statement join the others. A near-duplicate node silently splits the proposition.
  • Scope matters. It's one of the six grouping fields.
  • Features don't split. If you mark reversibility differently from another annotator on another paper, both statements still share a proposition; the disagreement shows up as variation within it.

The assessment edge

CAMO's EvidenceBaseAssessmentEdge is replaced by SynthesisAssessment. It's a statement made by the synthesis pipeline or a review team about a proposition, and its evidence lines cite statements (your annotations) as evidence items.

CAMO assessment edgeWARP
subject, predicate, objectproposition (the assessment targets a proposition, which has them)
included_edge_idsevidence lines' evidence_item_refs
aggregate_certainty_gradestrength (required, CertaintyGradeEnum)
evidence_balanceevidence_balance
(implicit)direction (required): supports, disputes or neutral
assessed_by, assessment_timestampan author contribution with a date
counts, Russo–Williamson and Bradford Hill fields, effect-size summaries, refresh tracking, FCM weightnot mapped yet

One new pattern: a token-level statement can be evidence for a type-level proposition. A practitioner's one-plot observation has its own assertional proposition, and a synthesis of the prototypical proposition can cite it as a (weak) evidence line. CAMO had no clean way to say this.

Entirely new things

statementText
A normalized rendering of the claim, alongside the verbatim original_text.
dateAuthored
When the author made the statement (publication year), distinct from when you recorded it.
Open questions
Propositions written before any statement exists, e.g. an empty evidence gap map cell. Your statement may turn one into an asserted proposition automatically.
Interaction statements
A second statement group for biotic interactions (eats, pollinates, parasite_of, biotically_interacts_with, from RO), using population nodes and the sign of the effect on each partner. Use these for "deer eat X" rather than forcing it into a causal predicate.
care
Consent, community, access level and Local Contexts labels. Required when a statement's author is an Indigenous knowledge holder or community member.
Practitioner statements
Field notebooks, interviews and practitioner reports can be annotated as statements with the practitioner as author, not only as evidence for published claims.
EcoStudyResult
A summary of one study arm (mean, SD, n) about one node, which evidence lines can point at.

Not mapped yet

Things you record in CAMO that have no WARP home yet. Keep recording them in CAMO for now, and tell the schema maintainers if you rely on them.

  • Study metadata on SourceDocument: study coordinates, country and province, study period and duration, study design, sample size, sampling effort. WARP's EcoDocument is a plain SEPIO document (title, DOI and so on).
  • annotation_confidence and annotation_notes.
  • Article-level certainty_grade: whether it becomes evidence-line strength or exists only on syntheses is undecided.
  • Most aggregate fields of the assessment edge.

Habits to change

  • Name the variable on nodes, with no state qualifier; let the predicate carry the sign.
  • Reuse existing node ids rather than making a near-duplicate.
  • Choose scope carefully: type is now prototypical, contingent or universal. Default to prototypical; reserve unclear for genuinely unclear sources.
  • Keep hedging (causal language) and scope separate.
  • Record the author as well as yourself.
  • Write one evidence line per evidence type, pairing each with its object, and give it a direction (and a strength, or not_assessed).
  • Keep filling causal_language and philosophical_accounts, though WARP doesn't force you to.
  • Never fill proposition or targetProposition.
  • Biotic interactions go in interaction statements.
  • Statements by Indigenous knowledge holders or community members need care.
